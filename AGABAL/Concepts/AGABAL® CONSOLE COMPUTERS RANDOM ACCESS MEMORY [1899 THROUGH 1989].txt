The Magnavox Odyssey
(released in 1972) is a hybrid analog and digital electronic system designed by Ralph Baer. It contains no microprocessor, CPU, or internal software/RAM, relying instead on discrete solid-state logic. 
Core Hardware Specifications

    Type / Architecture: Hybrid analog and digital circuit using DTL (diode-transistor logic) and 12 internal module/circuit boards. It has no programmable CPU or memory. 
    Processor & Memory: None. Game logic is hardwired into the console; interchangeable "game cards" act as external jumpers that open or close internal circuit paths. 
    Display / Graphics:
        Monochrome black-and-white output.
        Displays up to three square dots and one vertical line on the screen.
        Relies on translucent colored plastic screen overlays attached to the television to provide visual backgrounds and color context. 
    Audio: None. The console lacks a sound generator or speaker. 
    Controllers: Two wired rectangular controllers featuring three adjustment knobs (horizontal, vertical, and "English" / ball curve) plus a reset button. 
    Power Source: Operates on 9 volts, powered by either a wall adapter or six C-cell batteries. 
    Peripheral Support: Offered a separate Shooting Gallery light gun accessory (the first commercial light gun for a home console). 
    Analog / Board Accessories: Included non-electronic physical items like dice, paper money, poker chips, and board game score sheets. 

(Note: If you were looking for the second-generation, cartridge- and CPU-based Magnavox Odyssey²
from 1978, it used an Intel 8048 CPU at 1.79–5.91 MHz, 64 bytes of RAM, and an Intel 8244/8245 video chip supporting 160×200 resolution with 16 colors).
