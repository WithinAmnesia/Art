when was first electronic speaker invented?
The first crude electronic speaker was invented in 1861 by Johann Philipp Reis. 
Major milestones in the early development of the speaker include:

    1861: Johann Philipp Reis developed a crude electronic speaker as part of his telephone experiment, which could reproduce muffled noise. 
    1876: Alexander Graham Bell patented the first speech-reproducing loudspeaker as part of his telephone system. 
    1877: Werner von Siemens invented an early electrodynamic loudspeaker design. 
    1898: Oliver Lodge patented a moving-coil loudspeaker. 
    1915: Peter Laurits Jensen and Edwin Pridham built the Magnavox, the first practical electrodynamic loudspeaker. 
    1925: Edward W. Kellogg and Chester W. Rice invented the modern dynamic moving-coil loudspeaker, which set the standard for modern audio reproduction.
