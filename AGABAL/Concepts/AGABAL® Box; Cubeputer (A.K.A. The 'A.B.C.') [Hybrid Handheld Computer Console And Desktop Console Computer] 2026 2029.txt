An apostrophe (') is a punctuation mark used primarily to show possession or to indicate omitted letters in a contraction. 
Because your message only contained a single apostrophe, you might have sent it by accident, or you may be looking for information on how to use it properly. Here is a quick guide to its three main functions. 
1. Showing Possession (Ownership)
The rules change slightly depending on whether the noun is singular or plural: 

    Singular nouns: Add an apostrophe followed by an "s".
        Example: The dog’s leash. 
    Plural nouns ending in S: Add only the apostrophe at the very end.
        Example: The dogs’ leashes. 
    Plural nouns not ending in S: Add an apostrophe followed by an "s".
        Example: The children’s toys. 

2. Creating Contractions (Missing Letters)
When you combine two words to make a shorter one, the apostrophe stands in place of the dropped letters. 

    Do not becomes don't (replaces the "o").
    It is becomes it's (replaces the "i").
    We will becomes we'll (replaces the "wi"). 

3. Pluralizing Lowercase Letters (Rare Case)
The only time an apostrophe is used to make a plural is to avoid confusion when listing single lowercase letters. 

    Example: Remember to dot your i’s and cross your t’s.
