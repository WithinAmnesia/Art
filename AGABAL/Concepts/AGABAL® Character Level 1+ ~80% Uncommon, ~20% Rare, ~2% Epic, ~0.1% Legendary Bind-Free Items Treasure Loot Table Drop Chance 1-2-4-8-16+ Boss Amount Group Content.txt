The punctuation mark ' is an apostrophe (or a single quote symbol). In English grammar, it serves two primary functions: showing possession (ownership) and indicating omission (contractions). 
1. Showing Possession
The apostrophe indicates that a noun owns or is closely linked to something else: 

    Singular nouns: Add an apostrophe followed by an s (e.g., the cat's toy, Sarah's phone).
    Plural nouns ending in s: Add only the apostrophe at the end of the word (e.g., the parents' car, the dogs' food).
    Plural nouns not ending in s: Add an apostrophe and an s (e.g., the children's books, the men's team).
    Singular nouns ending in s: Depending on the style guide, you can add just the apostrophe or an apostrophe and an s (e.g., Chris' car or Chris's car). 

2. Forming Contractions (Omission)
The apostrophe takes the place of missing letters or numbers when words or dates are shortened: 

    Shortened words: Replaces omitted letters to combine words (e.g., do not becomes don't, it is becomes it's, I am becomes I'm).
    Shortened years: Replaces the century digits (e.g., 1999 becomes '99). 

3. Common Pitfalls to Avoid

    Plurals: Never use an apostrophe to make a standard noun plural (e.g., write two apples, not two apple's).
    Possessive Pronouns: Pronouns like its, hers, ours, theirs, and yours never take an apostrophe (e.g., its tail). Note that it's exclusively means "it is" or "it has".
