1. Showing Possession (Ownership)
The apostrophe shows that something belongs to someone or something else. 

    Singular nouns: Add an apostrophe followed by an s.
        Example: The cat's toy, Sarah's phone. 
    Plural nouns ending in s: Add only the apostrophe after the s.
        Example: The cats' food bowls, the kids' bedtime. 
    Irregular plural nouns (not ending in s): Add an apostrophe and an s.
        Example: The children's school, women's clothing. 
    Singular nouns ending in s: Most style guides prefer adding 's, though adding just the apostrophe is sometimes acceptable depending on the style guide you follow.
        Example: Chris's car or James' book. 

2. Creating Contractions (Omission)
When two words are combined to form a shorter word, the apostrophe stands in the exact place where letters were removed. 

    do not → don't (replaces the "o")
    it is → it's (replaces the "i")
    cannot → can't (replaces "no")
    they are → they're (replaces "a") 

Common Pitfalls to Avoid

    Do not use apostrophes for standard plurals: An apostrophe is never used to simply make a regular noun plural. Writing "apple's" when you mean multiple fruits is incorrect; it should be "apples". 
    Possessive Pronouns: Words like his, hers, ours, and its are already possessive and never take an apostrophe.
        Its = belonging to it (e.g., "The dog wagged its tail").
        It's = it is / it has (e.g., "It's raining outside").
