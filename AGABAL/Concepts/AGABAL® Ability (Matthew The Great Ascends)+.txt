The symbol ' is an apostrophe, a punctuation mark used in written English primarily for two reasons: showing when letters or numbers have been left out (contractions) and showing ownership (possession). It is also occasionally used to make single lowercase letters plural. 
1. Omission and Contractions
The apostrophe stands in place of missing letters when two words are combined into a shorter form. 

    do not becomes don't
    it is becomes it's
    1990s can be shortened to the '90s 

2. Showing Possession
It shows that something belongs to someone or something else. 

    Singular owner: Add 's (e.g., the dog's bone, Ben's car).
    Plural owners ending in -s: Add just the apostrophe at the very end (e.g., the twins' room, the cats' toys).
    Irregular plural owners: Add 's if the plural word doesn't end in -s (e.g., the children's shoes). 

3. Exceptional Plurals
Do not use an apostrophe to make regular words plural (e.g., apples, not apple's). However, it is used to prevent confusion when pluralizing lowercase letters. 

    Mind your p's and q's.
    Dot your i's and cross your t's.
