complexity
Complexity refers to the state or quality of a system composed of many interconnected, interacting parts that collectively exhibit behaviors that cannot be easily predicted by analyzing the individual components alone.
While a complicated system (like a commercial airplane) has many moving pieces that operate in a predictable, linear fashion, a complex system (like the global climate, the stock market, or human society) features adaptive elements that constantly react to one another, giving rise to unpredictable, non-linear outcomes. 
Core Traits of Complex Systems

    Emergence: The system as a whole exhibits properties or behaviors that none of its individual parts possess on their own (the whole is greater than the sum of its parts). 
    Feedback Loops: Actions cascade through the system and circle back to alter the original source, either accelerating (positive feedback) or dampening (negative feedback) a process. 
    Non-linearity: Small variations in one part of the system can cause massive, disproportionate disruptions elsewhere, often leading to sudden tipping points. 
    Self-Organization: In the absence of a central controller or leader, the individual parts interact locally to form organized, macro-level patterns over time.
