dev crunch is ass?
Dev crunch is widely considered harmful to both developers and the final game. While studios sometimes rely on it to hit strict release dates, rushing content almost always leaves visible seams—like reskinned battlegrounds, mismatched item levels, and unpolished zones.
Why Crunch Hurts the Game

    Cut Corners & Asset Reuse. Tight deadlines force teams to copy-paste existing layouts (like turning Eye of the Storm into Darkspear Islands) instead of building fresh environments from scratch.
    Leftover Design Seams. When plans change mid-development—such as cutting Battle for Gilneas and bumping Darkspear Islands from level 20 to 30—teams rarely have time to go back and fix the level 28 vendor gear or move out-of-place NPCs.
    More Bugs at Launch. Tired developers make more mistakes, leading to broken quests, unbalanced scaling, and clunky travel routes that have to be patched months later.

Why Crunch Hurts Developers

    Severe Burnout. Working 60–80 hour weeks for months drains creativity, harms mental and physical health, and pushes experienced designers out of the industry.
    Diminishing Returns. Studies in software development consistently show that productivity drops sharply after a few weeks of heavy overtime, meaning crunch often creates as many problems as it solves.

### What Happened to the Darkspear Islands Weapons (Beta vs. Launch)
1. **Cut Beta Vendor Items (October 2026 Wipe):**  
   During the September 2026 beta phases (capped at Level 20, then Level 30), players discovered the forward camps in The Barrens and inspected full vendor tables containing low-level usable PvP rewards.
