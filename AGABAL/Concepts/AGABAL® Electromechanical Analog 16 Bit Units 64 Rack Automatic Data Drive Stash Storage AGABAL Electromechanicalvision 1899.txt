The apostrophe (') is a punctuation mark used primarily to indicate possession (ownership) or the omission of letters (contractions). 
1. Showing Possession
To show that someone or something owns something, follow these basic rules: 

    Singular nouns: Add an apostrophe and an "s" (e.g., the dog's bone, Sarah's phone).
    Plural nouns ending in "s": Add just the apostrophe after the "s" (e.g., the cats' food bowls, the players' uniforms).
    Plural nouns not ending in "s": Add an apostrophe and an "s" (e.g., the children's school, the women's party).
    Possessive pronouns: Never use an apostrophe with words like its, hers, ours, yours, or theirs. 

2. Creating Contractions
When joining two words together into one, the apostrophe marks the spot where letters have been removed.

    do not becomes don't (omits the "o")
    it is becomes it's (omits the "i")
    we will becomes we'll (omits the "wi")
