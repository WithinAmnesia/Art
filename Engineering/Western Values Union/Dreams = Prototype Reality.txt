An apostrophe (') is a punctuation mark used primarily to indicate possession or to show the omission of letters in contractions. 
1. Indicating Possession

    Singular Nouns: Add an apostrophe followed by an "s" (e.g., the cat's toy, Sarah's phone).
    Plural Nouns Ending in S: Add only the apostrophe at the end of the word (e.g., the dogs' leashes, the parents' bedroom).
    Plural Nouns Not Ending in S: Add an apostrophe and an "s" (e.g., the children's toys, people's opinions). 

2. Omission of Letters (Contractions)

    Shortening Words: Replaces dropped letters when combining two words (e.g., do not becomes don't, it is becomes it's, I am becomes I'm). 

3. Plurals of Lowercase Letters

    Style Exception: Used rarely to make lowercase letters plural so they do not look like other words (e.g., Mind your p's and q's, Dot your i's).
