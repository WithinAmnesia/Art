An apostrophe (') is a punctuation mark used in writing to guide sentence structure, ownership, and clarity. 
The Core Roles of an Apostrophe
Apostrophes serve three primary purposes in the English language: 

    Showing Possession: It indicates that something belongs to someone or something else.
        Example: "Sarah's book" or "The cats' toys" (for multiple cats). 
    Creating Contractions: It stands in for missing letters or numbers when words are joined together.
        Example: "do not" becomes "don't", and "1995" can be shortened to "'95". 
    Pluralizing Single Letters: It clarifies plurals for lowercase letters so they aren't confused with words.
        Example: "Mind your p's and q's." 

Common Misconceptions

    Plurals: A standard plural noun does not need an apostrophe. Writing "apple's" instead of "apples" is a frequent error.
    Possessive Pronouns: Words that already show possession on their own—like its, his, hers, ours, yours, and theirs—never take an apostrophe.
